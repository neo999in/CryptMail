Encrypted mail, on the inbox you already have.

System architecture

One client, two backends it never has to trust

All cryptography happens on the device. The client talks to the user's mail provider (which only ever holds ciphertext) and to a thin CryptMail service (which only ever holds public keys and opaque backups).

Trust boundary. Cross either connector and you find only ciphertext, public keys, and passphrase-wrapped blobs. Neither the mail provider nor the CryptMail service can read message content or reconstruct a usable private key.
